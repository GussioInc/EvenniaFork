MULTI-CLASSING 

When you choose to multi-class, you give up a few of the more elite skills and 
spells, haste for example, in exchange for a much greater variety of abilities. 
Be forewarned that leveling a multi-class character is a difficult and time 
consuming process, so do not make the choice lightly.  It is also difficult to
practice all your skills and spells at low levels as a multi-class character, 
due to the greater variety you have available to you, and the fact that you
do NOT receive more practices than a standard single class character.


Dual-classing involves a combination of two classes from cleric, illusionist, 
magic-user, thief and warrior depending on what your race allows.  These
classes are similar to the priest, wizard, rogue and swordsman classes in
abilities but, as said, do not possess ALL of the skills from their more
advanced counterparts.  Such a dual class is written Wa/Mu, for example,
denoting a Warrior/Magic-User combo.


Tri-classing is similar to dual-classing.  It, obviously, involves three of
the above classes instead of two, and makes it harder still to level.  A
tri-class character is written W/T/C, for example.

When leveling a multi-class character, you gain two or three times as many
levels total, but your hp, mn and mv only increase by a little bit each level.
This means by the time you hit 109/109/109, your hp, mn and mv will be some-
what averaged between the values for your classes taken separately.


To view the skills and spells available to you as a dual- or tri-class 
character, use a short string composed of the first letters of your selected
classes, in the order: W, T, C, M, I.  For example:
